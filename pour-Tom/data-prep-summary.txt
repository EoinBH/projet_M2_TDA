TEMPORAL PROJECT – DATA PREPARATION SUMMARY

1. PROJECT OBJECTIVE

The project aims to forecast French metropolitan electricity consumption for all 24 hours of day J+1, using information available at 14:00 on day J.

The first stage consists of constructing a clean, reproducible hourly dataset covering 2022–2025, combining:

- RTE: electricity consumption, production and cross-border exchanges.
- Météo-France: SYNOP meteorological observations.
- Calendar data: public holidays, school holidays and temporal characteristics.

The dataset will support exploratory analysis, time-series analysis and forecasting model development.

A central methodological requirement is to prevent information leakage: predictors must only use information genuinely available at the forecast origin.

2. DATA AND METHODOLOGICAL DECISIONS

**Electricity data – RTE**
- Four annual definitive datasets (2022–2025) were selected.
- The definitive data were preferred to provisional observations for consistency and reliability.
- Original 15-minute power observations are aggregated into hourly averages. Power (MW) must be averaged, not summed.
- Consumption, production by energy source, exchanges and CO2 intensity are retained where available.

**Weather data – Météo-France SYNOP**
- SYNOP was selected as the meteorological source because it provides observed weather measurements from a network of stations.
- A national spatial approach was chosen: observations from metropolitan French stations are aggregated into national indicators.
- Original three-hourly observations are preserved, alongside hourly interpolated variables.
- Circular averaging is used for wind direction because directions are angular measurements.
- Interpolated weather observations are identified so that observed and estimated values remain distinguishable.

Important forecasting distinction: observed SYNOP weather for J+1 is not available at 14:00 on J. It can support retrospective analysis or a perfect-weather benchmark, but an operational model needs archived weather forecasts available at the prediction origin.

**Calendar data**
- Public holidays are imported from JSON and school holidays from ICS.
- School holiday zones A, B and C are retained separately because France does not have a single uniform school holiday calendar.
- Weekends, weekdays, seasons, holiday eves and cyclical time features are generated.
- Calendar information is known in advance and is therefore suitable for operational forecasting.

**Timestamp convention**
- UTC is the common reference for merging datasets.
- RTE observations are interpreted in Europe/Paris local time.
- SYNOP timestamps are interpreted in UTC.
- Calendar features are calculated using French local time.
- Both UTC and local timestamps are retained to handle daylight-saving transitions correctly, including the missing spring hour and repeated autumn hour.

3. PROJECT STRUCTURE AND PIPELINE

The project is organised into raw data, processed data and R scripts. Raw files remain unchanged.

Five scripts automate the preparation:

- `00_run_pipeline.R`: installs dependencies, loads packages, creates directories and runs the pipeline.
- `01_prepare_RTE.R`: imports, cleans and aggregates RTE electricity data.
- `02_prepare_weather.R`: imports, filters, aggregates and interpolates SYNOP data.
- `03_prepare_calendar.R`: constructs public holiday, school holiday and temporal variables.
- `04_merge_datasets.R`: merges the datasets using UTC, reconstructs local timestamps and generates diagnostics.

4. FINAL DATASET

Main output: `data/processed/modelling_dataset.csv`

It contains:

- Electricity: consumption, forecasts, production by source, exchanges, CO2 intensity and other available indicators.
- Weather: temperature, dew point, humidity, wind, visibility, pressure and precipitation.
- Calendar: date, hour, weekday, weekend, public holidays, school holidays, zones and season.
- Cyclical features: sine and cosine transformations of hour, weekday and month.

Intermediate outputs include `rte_hourly.csv`, `weather_3hourly.csv`, `weather_hourly.csv` and `calendar_hourly.csv`.

5. DATA QUALITY RESULTS

The first complete pipeline execution produced:

- 35,064 unique hourly observations.
- First timestamp: 2021-12-31 23:00 UTC.
- Last timestamp: 2025-12-31 22:00 UTC.
- Missing electricity consumption: 4 observations (approximately 0.011%).
- Missing temperature: 62 observations (approximately 0.177%).
- Missing calendar observations: 0.

Other findings:
- Short-period temperature extrema (`tn12`, `tx12`) have approximately 91.5% missingness.
- Solar radiation (`sw`) is entirely missing.
- The station-count variable is missing for approximately 66.7% of hours because SYNOP observations are three-hourly and this variable is not interpolated.
- Public holiday names are missing on most dates, as expected.

Variables with substantial or complete missingness should be investigated before modelling.

6. REMAINING VALIDATION

Before beginning statistical modelling:

1. Verify RTE repeated-hour ordering during the autumn clock change.
2. Validate school holiday extraction for zones A, B and C, including Corsica where applicable.
3. Confirm SYNOP measurement units and missing-value codes against the technical documentation.
4. Investigate the four missing RTE consumption observations.
5. Investigate the 62 missing temperature observations.
6. Understand why solar radiation is entirely missing.
7. Review variables with substantial missingness and decide whether to retain, transform or exclude them.
8. Check station coverage and the stability of national weather averages.
9. Validate timestamp continuity, duplicates and the treatment of daylight-saving transitions.

7. IMPORTANT NEXT METHODOLOGICAL STEP

The dataset is technically assembled, but it is not yet automatically suitable for operational forecasting.

The original prediction scenario must guide feature selection:

- Forecast origin: 14:00 on day J.
- Prediction horizon: all 24 hours of day J+1.
- Historical consumption values must respect their actual availability at 14:00 on J.
- Future observed weather must not be used as an operational predictor.
- Calendar variables are safe because they are known in advance.
- Validation must be chronological, not based on random train/test splitting.

The distinction between operational forecasting and retrospective perfect-weather modelling must be maintained.

8. REPRODUCIBILITY AND CURRENT STATUS

The complete preparation pipeline is automated and can be executed from the project root using:

`source("R/00_run_pipeline.R")`

The pipeline imports the raw data, prepares each source, merges the datasets and generates diagnostic files.

**Status:** Initial data preparation and merging are complete. The project is now at the data validation and feature-selection stage, before exploratory analysis and forecasting model development.
